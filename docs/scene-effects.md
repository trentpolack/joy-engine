# Scene Effects
`Renderer::set_scene_effects` configures the effects that run on the HDR scene before bloom and the display transform: temporal antialiasing, motion blur, adaptive exposure, and volumetric fog and lighting. Each is off (`None`) or on with its settings in `rendering::scene_effects::SceneEffectsOptions`, and `validate` names any setting outside its range. `development::SceneEffectsControls` exposes temporary preview controls, and the [scene-effects example](../examples/scene-effects/README.md) isolates them in one room. Effects remain opt-in and never rewrite authored assets.

## Effects
- **Temporal antialiasing** jitters the raster projection by a Halton (2, 3) sequence and blends each pixel with its history, reprojected by motion vectors and clipped to the variance of the current neighborhood in YCoCg so moving and disoccluded content does not ghost. History is filtered with Catmull-Rom and blended with brightness-weighted shares, so highlights neither smear nor flicker.
- **Motion blur** reconstructs camera and object blur from the motion vectors (McGuire et al. 2012): tile-max and neighbor-max passes find the dominant motion, and a depth-aware gather lets moving foreground smear over the background without the background bleeding across it. `shutter` is the fraction of a frame the shutter stays open; `maxLength` caps the blur as a fraction of the shorter viewport axis.
- **Adaptive exposure** meters the scene and moves the exposure toward the value that maps the metered average to `keyValue` (middle gray), brightening and darkening at separate rates per second within `minimumExposure` to `maximumExposure`. With compute shaders a 256-bin log-luminance histogram meters the scene, ignoring the darkest `lowPercentile` and the brightest light above `highPercentile`; on WebGL 2 a center-weighted log average is reduced through small float targets instead. The result is a one-texel exposure texture the display chain multiplies with the authored exposure.
- **Volumetric fog and lighting** fills a froxel volume aligned with the view (cells a few pixels wide, slices spaced by the square root of depth) with exponential height fog, the light it scatters from the key light, point lights, and the skylight (Henyey-Greenstein phase), and volumetric shadows from the shadow maps, blended with the previous frame's volume. Integration along each view ray (Hillaire 2015) yields scattered light and transmittance, which a full-screen pass applies over opaque surfaces and the sky, and transparent surfaces apply to themselves. `quality` picks the grid; large drawing buffers widen the cells so the volume stays bounded.

Local exposure is not implemented yet; adaptive exposure is global.

## Frame Contracts
With temporal antialiasing or motion blur, opaque materials write a motion attachment beside their color in the passes that draw them (the G-buffer and forward passes) (screen-space motion from the previous frame without jitter, view depth, and coverage), so opaque geometry is rasterized once and no effect loads the depth buffer. Every instance carries its previous model matrix and the renderer keeps the previous camera; history resets on camera cuts, presentation-clock jumps longer than a quarter second, projection changes, and `Renderer::reset_history`. The sky, which writes no motion, reprojects through the camera change.

Per frame: the shadow views render, volumetric injection and integration run in compute (reading the frame's light clusters), then the depth pre-pass and the opaque passes ([lighting and shadows](lighting.md)); fog is applied over opaque surfaces before transparency draws (fogging itself through the frame group's volume); then temporal antialiasing, motion blur over the antialiased image, and adaptive exposure metering the result. Transparent surfaces write no motion, so they reproject with what lies behind them.

## Backends
Every effect runs on WebGPU and native backends. WebGL 2 runs temporal antialiasing, motion blur, and adaptive exposure where float render targets are available, and volumetric fog, which needs compute shaders, is skipped. `Renderer::scene_effects_capabilities` reports which requested effects run and why the others do not, and the preview controls show that reason, so a missing effect is never silent.

Fog and volumetric light are rendering effects rather than gameplay simulation. Screen-space motion cannot see offscreen sources. Keep these limits visible when judging captures.

## Verification
Run engine integration tests, the example's native and browser builds, and captures on the affected backends. Headless rendering tests in `tests/integration/rendering_scene_effects.rs` check that each effect changes the image the way it should (and leaves still frames alone where it should), that WebGL 2 falls back without volumetric fog, and that invalid settings are refused; unit tests validate every effect shader and translate the WebGL 2 passes. Inspect temporal behavior interactively as well as still captures; CPU timing and frame cadence do not establish GPU execution time.
